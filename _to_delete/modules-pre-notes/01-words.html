Module 1

How the machine makes words

A 1948 idea, running at industrial scale.

Thesis: the idea behind LLMs is from 1948. What changed is scale, data and architecture. By the end of this module they should see the machine as a sampler, not an oracle.

1948 — language as a game of probability

  • Shannon: text is a source that emits symbols with a probability.
  • Sampling from those statistics gives sentences that sound English and say nothing.
  • 1951: he measures how predictable English is — 0.6–1.3 bits per letter.
  • An LLM estimates the same distribution: learned, not counted by hand.
THE HEAD AND IN FRONTAL ATTACK ON AN ENGLISH WRITER THAT THE CHARACTER OF THIS POINT IS THEREFORE ANOTHER METHOD FOR THE LETTERS THAT THE TIME OF WHO EVER TOLD THE PROBLEM FOR AN UNEXPECTED. Text generated by Shannon in 1948 by sampling word-pair statistics.
Fluent and empty: the machine was already doing that in 1948.
Sources: Shannon (1948) A Mathematical Theory of Communication, approximations to English; Shannon (1951) Prediction and Entropy of Printed English — 0.6–1.3 bits per letter, measured with 100 letters of context. It is the one number in this module worth saying out loud. Read the 1948 sample aloud: it sounds English and says nothing. That is the literal ancestor of hallucination - we come back to it in module 5. An LLM is a very large learned estimator of the same distribution; everything else is engineering around that one operation. Do not put the formula on screen - the widget two slides from now does the work.

From rules to learned patterns

Three jumps: from rules to data, from data to vectors, from completion to instruction.
Two minutes, no more: the timeline is a list of technical choices, not a story. Three stops worth saying out loud. 2013 (Mikolov): words become vectors, closeness in that space is similarity of meaning - the mechanism that comes back in module 3 as retrieval. 2017 (Vaswani): attention compares every word with every other word in parallel, which makes training at scale practical; do not open the encoder-decoder. 2022 (Ouyang, InstructGPT): training with human feedback adds instruction-following on top of plain text completion - the difference between a model that continues a sentence and the assistant they use daily, and the reason module 4 works at all. Sources: Mikolov (2013); Vaswani (2017); Brown (2020); Ouyang (2022).

Tokens, not words

  • The machine never sees letters: it sees sub-word pieces, chosen by frequency in the data.
  • Rule of thumb: 1 token ≈ 4 characters ≈ 3/4 of a word (in English).
  • The token is the unit of price and of memory — and languages do not cost the same.
Hover or tap a token to see how frequent it is.

It does not fail out of stupidity, it fails because the letters never reach it. The Italian/English comparison is the moment an owner cares about: the same sentence is 14 tokens in Italian and 6 in English (3.4 vs 7.0 characters per token). Same meaning, more than twice the cost and twice the context consumed. Sources: Sennrich et al. (2016) for BPE; OpenAI (1 token ~ 4 characters); Petrov et al. (NeurIPS 2023) for the gap between languages.

One operation, repeated

  • It works out the odds of the next piece, picks one, adds it, starts again.
  • What it writes becomes part of what it reads: early choices steer every later one.
  • It consults no archive — it chooses, one piece at a time.

Temperature: the randomness dial

Low · 0.2–0.5

The distribution tightens onto the most likely candidate.

→ extraction, classification, repeatable output

High · 1.0–2.0

The distribution flattens: rare candidates get in.

→ variants, rewrites, brainstorming

Always

It redistributes probability, it adds no knowledge. At T = 0 a wrong answer just becomes a repeatable wrong answer.
