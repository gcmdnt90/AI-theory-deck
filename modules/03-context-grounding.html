Context, memory, grounding

How the machine gets to know your business.

The module with the highest direct business value. Core distinction: context is what is in front of the model right now; memory is what survives between sessions. Both are "around".

The context window

Context is the model's desk: everything it can see while answering this one question.

RulesYour instructions sit at the front of the desk.
Conversation + documentsThey compete for the same finite space.
Room to answerThe reply needs space on the desk too.
The desk is wiped after every question unless you put things back on it.
The wiped-desk image is the one they must keep. It explains why a fresh chat "forgets", why long threads degrade, and why the project/instruction file in Module 4 matters.

A bigger desk is not a free desk

Windows grew from about 1,500 words to book length in a few years.

But what sits at the beginning and the end is used better than what is buried in the middle.

What you put in, and in what order, still matters.
Counter the "just paste everything in" reflex before they develop it. Source: Liu et al. (2023/24), Lost in the Middle, TACL. If asked for numbers: GPT-3 was ~2k tokens, current frontier models reach ~1M, roughly a million and a half words. Curation still beats dumping.

Words become positions on a map of meanings — so "search by meaning" finds the right passage even when the wording differs.

Your documents are not trained into the model. They are handed to it, one question at a time.
The most useful pattern this audience will actually adopt. Land two consequences: (1) nothing is memorised, so updating a document updates the answers immediately; (2) the model can only answer from what retrieval found, so retrieval quality IS answer quality. The embedding video earns its place here: same maths as the map of meanings, used as a search index. Sources: Lewis et al. (2020); Karpukhin et al. (2020); Mikolov et al. (2013).
