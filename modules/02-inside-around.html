Module 2

Inside the model, around the model

Two things people constantly confuse.

The single most useful conceptual correction in the course. Almost every "can AI do X?" question is really a question about which side of this boundary X sits on.

The boundary

Inside — the processor

What the model learned during training.

→ it does not change: nobody retrains a frontier model

Around — the scaffolding

Memory, documents, tools, rules, loops.

→ it is built today, and it can be undone

What is “needed” is almost always around it.

The test: does it change the model itself, or only what the model is given?
The good news for an SME is entirely on the right-hand card: you cannot retrain a frontier model, and you do not need to. Everything that will make this useful for their business is "around" - and around is cheap, fast and reversible. Say that explicitly; it reframes the whole afternoon from "expensive AI project" to "configuration".

How a model is made

Yesterday — the only one we know in full

1. Reads textPredicts the next word across enormous amounts of writing.300 billion tokens
2. Learns to follow instructionsExamples teach it to carry out a task, not just continue text.~13,000 hand-written examples
3. Tuned by human feedbackPeople judge the answers; the model is pushed towards the preferred ones.~33,000 comparisons, ~40 people

Today

Llama 3: ~15 trillion tokens at step 1, fifty times InstructGPT — and a growing share of the examples in steps 2 and 3 is generated by other models, not written by people.

Knowledge comes from step 1. Assistant behaviour comes from steps 2 and 3.
Common belief in the room: "the model is one trained thing that knows facts". Correct it here, and correct yourself too: the three numbers belong to ONE specific model - InstructGPT, 2022, the ancestor of ChatGPT, built on top of GPT-3. It is the only case in that family with a full public account of all three steps. Other models differ by orders of magnitude: the shape of the pipeline holds, the figures do not. The "Today" strip is the honest line of this module: it tells them what they are learning has an expiry date. If time is short, skip it aloud and leave it on screen. Two hooks to keep in your pocket. First: step 3 pushes the model towards what the raters liked - that is where sycophancy comes from, not from a wish to please them; you collect it in Module 5. Second: synthetic data is the same thing we do when we generate the fake sheets for today's demos. Sources: Brown (2020) for GPT-3; Ouyang (2022) for the three figures; Llama 3 (2024) for the "Today" strip; Christiano (2017) for human feedback.

"Reasoning": trained or prompted

inside Trained

Trained with reinforcement learning to deliberate before answering: self-checking and second thoughts emerge from the training, not from an instruction.

→ the “thinking” versions of ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Copilot; DeepSeek-R1 is the one with a public account

around Prompted

It is asked for in the prompt: “list the criteria first, then decide”.

→ free, on any model, including the free ones

The switch injects no prompt: it turns on a mode of the model — and today the model itself decides how much to think. Written instructions, task lists and loops do exist, but they are scaffolding: module 4.

The visible steps are produced text, not the minutes of how it got there.

What scrolls past on screen is a model-generated summary, not the internal trace (OpenAI).

Two of these are reasoning, the third is not: the room conflates all three. (1) PROMPTED: you ask in the prompt, the model writes the steps as ordinary output; nothing about the model changes. (2) SCAFFOLDING: system prompt, instruction and personality files read on every request, task lists, loops, tools - it is real, but it is module 4, and it is not what makes a model a reasoning model. (3) TRAINED: reinforcement learning, the ability sits in the weights. DeepSeek-R1 shows it publicly: pure RL, no human-written reasoning traces, and self-reflection, verification and strategy switching emerge from training. OpenAI says the same of o1: "our large-scale reinforcement learning algorithm teaches the model how to think productively using its chain of thought". If asked what the button does: it injects no instructions, it turns on a technical mode. On Claude it was a thinking-token budget (thinking parameter + budget_tokens, up to 4.6); from 4.7 that parameter is gone, replaced by adaptive thinking with an effort level - the model decides whether and how much to think. On ChatGPT, picking the "thinking" version routes to a different model. Version numbers age in weeks: keep them here, not on screen, and check the deck edition date. Why the takeaway: Turpin et al. (2023) planted a biasing cue in the input (for instance the correct answer always in the same position); models followed the cue, got it wrong, and never mentioned the cue in their chain - they built a plausible justification for the wrong answer, up to -36% accuracy across 13 tasks. The cue is the measuring instrument, not advice for the audience: keep it off screen. This slide is the first deposit of the vigilance you collect in M5 ("fluency is not proof of accuracy"). Demo m2 lands it: the switch is the trained kind, "list the criteria first" is the prompted kind. Sources: DeepSeek-R1 (2025); OpenAI, Learning to reason with LLMs (2024); Turpin et al. (2023); Wei et al. (2022); Nye et al. (2021).

An agent = model + loop + tools + memory

LoopDecides, acts, observes the result, decides again.the model decides when to stop: that is why it can spin in circles
ToolsFunctions described in words, which the model chooses whether to call.files, search, email, databases — module 5
MemoryWhat is put back in front of it on the next turn.how it actually works: module 3
The difference between a chatbot and an agent is the scaffolding around the same model, not a bigger model.
From here on everything is "around". The model deliberately has no card of its own: it is what the whole module has been about, and the takeaway says so. Say the loop out loud - decides, acts, observes - because you will narrate exactly those three words over the Cowork demo in Module 4, and they need to recognise them when they hear them again. The two forward references keep nobody hanging: tools open in M4, memory in M3. If someone asks about cost straight away, the answer is in the first card: the loop decides for itself when to stop, so a badly specified agent can repeat useless turns - which is why M4 writes the spec first and verifies after. Sources: Yao et al. (2023) ReAct; Schick et al. (2023).
