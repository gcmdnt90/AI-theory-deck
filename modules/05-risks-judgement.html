Module 5

Risks and judgement

What goes wrong — in the model, in us, in the data.

Three risk owners: the model, the user, the data. That structure should survive even if every example is forgotten. Then close on who decides.

model What the model gets wrong

Hallucination

Fluent, confident, false. Structural, not a bug. It can be reduced — not removed.

Bias

Skews in the training text carried through into the answer.

Agreeableness

Agrees rather than correcting. It was trained to please.

Fluency is evidence of pattern mastery, never proof of truth.

All three trace back to Module 1: it optimises for plausible text, not for true text.

Connect causally to the engine they saw in Module 1: sampling from a distribution with no truth oracle attached. That is why hallucination is manageable but not eliminable, and why the grounding ladder in Module 3 reduces it rather than solving it. The "stochastic parrot" provocation (Bender et al. 2021) belongs here if someone raises it - present it as a live debate, not a verdict. Sources: Ji et al. (2023); Perez et al. (2022); Sharma et al. (2023).

model Jagged, not human

Jagged ability

Brilliant at one task, hopeless at a neighbouring one that looks just as hard. There is no manual for which is which.

The car-wash test

A model that can rewrite a hundred thousand lines of code may tell you to walk to the car wash 50 m away.

A colleagueKnows what they don't know. Asks. Adapts under pressure.
An agentCompletes the pattern. Confidently blind to what is missing.
SoManage it by specifying and checking — not by trusting and delegating.
The edges are found empirically, on real work.
The fourth model-side risk, after hallucination, bias and agreeableness: it does not fail where you expect. Karpathy: "We're not building animals. We're building ghosts" - statistical simulations of human text, not evolved creatures. The car-wash example is his own. Measured version: a field experiment with 758 BCG consultants (Dell'Acqua, Mollick et al., Organization Science) found AI raised quality inside the frontier and DEGRADED it outside, on tasks of similar apparent difficulty - 19 points less likely to be correct. Karpathy's metaphor, Mollick's data. This is the practitioner's version of the anti-anthropomorphism thread from Module 1, and the reason Module 4 closed on spec and verification rather than on trust.

user What happens to us

Skill erosion

Offloading the thinking can erode the very skill that was offloaded.

The fluency trap

Polished output feels more accurate than it is — especially outside one's own expertise.

The question enters the answer

An assumption placed inside the question comes back as if it were established. "Why is it rarer?" does not check that it is.

The risk is not only a wrong answer. It is losing the ability to notice one.
Make this concrete for a small firm: the expert in the room can spot a bad attribution instantly. A junior using the same tool cannot, and may never develop the eye if the tool always answers first. That is a staffing and training decision, not a technology one. Third card: it closes the thread opened in Module 3 with "holidays" versus "annual leave". There the wording moved the retrieval; here the assumption moves the generation, because the prompt is the text the model continues. The operational rule to dictate: ask "what is missing from this attribution?", not "confirm this attribution is right". Sources: Risko & Gilbert (2016); Lee & See (2004); Lee et al. (2025).

data Where data leaks

Cloud or local

Who can see the questions and the files uploaded?

Prompt injection

Text inside a document quietly becomes an instruction.

The dangerous moment is when untrusted text stops being data and starts steering an action.
Free appCheapest. Weakest guarantees about reuse.
SubscriptionBusiness terms, data not used for training.
Paid APIContractual control, the company's own logging.
Local modelNothing leaves the building. Weaker answers.

In the EU the AI Act (Reg. 2024/1689) and the GDPR both apply whichever option is chosen.

Connectors from Module 4 widen the attack surface: the agent now acts on untrusted input. Injection has no clean fix - design for least privilege and keep destructive actions in the "never do" tier. The four options are a real procurement decision for them, not a warning: the demo that follows opens by running the M3 question on a fully local model with the network unplugged, so they can feel the trade rather than be told about it. Sources: Greshake et al. (2023); OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications.

The human in the loop

Working loop safe

A person sets the goal, reviews what matters, and carries the responsibility.

Broken loop risk

A person rubber-stamps. Oversight exists on paper only.

Oversight is real only if someone can understand, challenge and overrule.
Automation complacency: oversight degrades precisely because the system is usually right. For an auction house the point is sharp - responsibility toward buyers cannot be delegated to a model, legally or reputationally. They have just watched the expert overrule it live.
