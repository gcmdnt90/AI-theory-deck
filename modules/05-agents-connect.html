Reaching your systems — and how to drive without crashing.

Two halves: how it connects (connectors), and how a serious practitioner actually works with it (spec, verify, guardrails). The second half is the part that survives contact with their job.

One socket instead of many

Without a standardEvery assistant-to-tool pair needs its own custom wiring.
With MCPAssistants and tools speak one shared language of request and reply.
Not a mergeIt moves requests and results. It does not pool your data.
MCP is the USB-C of AI tooling: one plug, many devices.
Open standard from Anthropic, now widely adopted. Do not go near the specification. The line that matters commercially: their existing systems do not have to be rebuilt to be reachable. Sources: anthropic.com/news/model-context-protocol; modelcontextprotocol.io.

Jagged, not human

Jagged ability

Brilliant at one task, hopeless at a neighbouring one that looks just as hard. There is no manual for which is which.

The car-wash test

A model that can rewrite a hundred thousand lines of code may tell you to walk to the car wash 50 m away.

A colleagueKnows what they don't know. Asks. Adapts under pressure.
An agentCompletes the pattern. Confidently blind to what is missing.
SoManage it by specifying and checking — not by trusting and delegating.
Find the edges empirically, on your own work.
Karpathy: "We're not building animals. We're building ghosts" - statistical simulations of human text, not evolved creatures. The car-wash example is his own. Measured version: a field experiment with 758 BCG consultants (Dell'Acqua, Mollick et al., Organization Science) found AI raised quality inside the frontier and DEGRADED it outside, on tasks of similar apparent difficulty - 19 points less likely to be correct. Karpathy's metaphor, Mollick's data. This is the practitioner's version of the anti-anthropomorphism thread from Module 1.

Own the spec, then verify

1. Own the spec

The agent fills in blanks; you own the plan. Say what the work must let you decide, not just what to produce.

2. Verify against something outside

Decide what "correct" looks like before it generates. Then check against the source document, not against its own opinion.

Guardrails by risk, not by politeness. Written rules are suggestions the model can override. Real limits live outside it.

Always doRoutine, low risk — runs on its own.
Ask firstModerate risk — confirmation required before acting.
Never doDestructive or irreversible — blocked at the tool, out of the model's reach.
The generator is cheap. The check is the craft.
Karpathy's automation thesis: traditional software automates what you can specify; LLMs automate what you can VERIFY - so make your own work verifiable. Cherny (creator of Claude Code): "Give Claude a way to verify its work... it will 2-3x the quality" - a practitioner's estimate, say so. Shankar et al. (UIST 2024) on criteria drift: grading real outputs changes your criteria, so spec and verification feed each other; it is one loop, not a pipeline. Cautionary tale worth telling verbally: Karpathy's agent matched payments to accounts by email address instead of a persistent user ID - plausible code, wrong system design, caught only by human judgement.
